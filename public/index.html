📐 KPI Formula Reference (click to expand/collapse)

Today's Consumption
= Σ demand readings where date = today
7-Day / 30-Day Avg
= Σ daily totals ÷ number of days with data
Avg L / Patient / Day
= Demand (L) ÷ Inpatient count (per reading)

WHO adequate: 100–300 L/patient/day
Peak Hour
= Hour-of-day with highest avg demand rate (L/hr)
Rate = volume drop ÷ Δhrs between readings
Spike Events
= Days where daily total > 130% of 7-day rolling avg
Peak Day
= Calendar date with highest Σ demand across all tanks
Low-Level Alerts
= Readings where Level% ≤ user-set alert threshold
DOH guidance: ≤20% = Low · ≤10% = Critical
Volume (L)
= Tank Capacity (L) × Level% ÷ 100
Demand (L) — 40 GPM Pump Model
= Pump Delivered − Volume Delta
Pump Delivered = 6,339 L/hr × Δhrs
Tank rising = demand < pump · Tank falling = demand > pump

Hospital Asset Maintenance Monitoring System (HAMMS) — Version 2.6.0
A comprehensive facilities management system for the General Services Division. Covers Work Orders, Asset Registry with PMS scheduling, Energy & Carbon Estimates, Safety Issue Monitoring, Project Tracking, EMB Waste Compliance, Materials Inventory, Water Safety, and Water Supply Monitoring with DOH/WHO-referenced demand analytics — with Firebase as the live operational data source for synchronized records.
📋 Work Orders 🔩 Asset Registry + PMS ⚡ Energy & Carbon 🦺 Safety Management 🏗 Project Monitoring 💧 Water Safety 🪣 Water Supply Monitoring 📦 Inventory ♻ EMB Waste

📍 Section 1 — Where to Run the System

HAMMS is a single HTML file. It requires no local installer and uses Firebase as its live operational data source.

✅ Recommended: Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge (Chromium)
  1. Save HAMMS.html anywhere on your laptop (Desktop or Documents).
  2. Open the file in Chrome or Edge.
  3. Sign in to Firebase when prompted so the system can load the shared live dataset.
  4. Watch the top bar for LIVE, or offline status indicators.
  5. Use JSON export only for optional manual backup or transfer copy, not as the live operating database.
✅ Live Data Mode:
  1. Firebase Cloud (primary and only live source): Shared operational data is loaded from and saved to Firebase.
  2. Real-time synchronization: Updates from other signed-in devices can be reflected in the app without switching storage modes.
  3. Manual backup: JSON export remains available only as an optional backup / transfer utility.
⚠ Important Rules:
  • Use a stable internet connection whenever possible for the smoothest Firebase synchronization.
  • Do NOT use Incognito / Private mode if you want the normal signed-in app workflow.
  • Topbar chip now reflects Firebase Live, Firebase Syncing, or Firebase Offline only.

💾 Section 2 — Transferring to Another Computer

✅ Simple Transfer — Use the Same Firebase Account
  1. Copy HAMMS.html to the new computer if needed.
  2. Open HAMMS in Chrome or Edge.
  3. Sign in using the authorized Firebase account.
  4. Use Reload from Firebase or check the Firebase status button if you need to verify the live connection.
  5. Use exported JSON only as a manual archive or recovery copy.
🚫 Do not treat JSON backups as the live database. The HTML is the application; Firebase is the operational source of truth.

🪣 Water Supply Monitoring — v2.3

Log water level readings per tank. The system automatically calculates volume, demand, demand rate, and liters per patient. No asset registry link required.

How to log a reading:
  1. Click ＋ Log Level Reading.
  2. Enter Date & Time (auto-filled to now, editable).
  3. Enter the Tank / Reservoir Name — same name every time for the same tank. This is how readings are grouped.
  4. Enter Tank Capacity (L) — full volume at 100%. Auto-filled from your last reading for that tank name.
  5. Set a Low-Level Alert % (DOH recommends ≥20% reserve).
  6. Enter Current Water Level %. Live bar and volume label appear instantly.
  7. Confirm Inpatient Count (auto-filled from dashboard census).
  8. Save. Volume, demand, rate, and L/patient are all calculated automatically.
Auto-calculated fields:
Field Formula
Volume (L)Capacity × Level% ÷ 100
Demand (L)Previous volume − Current volume (positive = consumed)
Rate (L/hr)Demand ÷ Hours between readings
L / PatientDaily demand ÷ Inpatient count
Refill / BaselineVolume rose >50 L = Refill flagged, excluded from demand. First reading = Baseline.
Charts:
  • Forecast Line Graph — Volume (L) blue line, Inpatient count green line, 72-hour projected depletion red dashed line. Accounts for pump inflow (139.3 LPM). Alert lines at 20% and 10% capacity. Estimated low-level date annotated on chart.
  • Demand Rate by Hour of Day — average L/hr per hour across all readings. Peak hour in red. Build by taking readings at different shifts.
  • Daily Consumption vs Inpatients — scatter with regression line once multiple days exist.

💧 Section 6 — DOH & WHO Water Standards Reference

The Water Supply Monitoring module applies the following official standards automatically as system alerts.

Standard Value Source
Minimum water / inpatient / day100 LWHO HCWM Guidelines
Adequate water / inpatient / day100–300 LWHO
Safe drinking water residual chlorine0.2–0.5 mg/LDOH AO 2007-0012
Safe water distribution pressure7–70 psiDOH Hospital Facility Standards
Low-level alert threshold≤ 20% capacityDOH Engineering Division
Critical low level≤ 10% capacityOperational threshold
How flags are applied:
🔴 L/patient <100 → "Below WHO minimum" — investigate distribution losses
✅ L/patient 100–300 → "Within WHO range" — supply is adequate
🟡 L/patient >300 → "Above WHO ceiling" — possible leak or overcounting
⚠ Tank level ≤ alert% → Low Level flag on reading, gauge bar turns yellow/red
🔴 Projected line crosses 20% → estimated low-level date shown on forecast

⚡ Section 7 — Energy Management & Carbon Footprint

Formulas:
  • Connected Load (kW) = Rated Watts ÷ 1,000
  • Monthly kWh = kW × Operating Hours/Day × Operating Days/Month × Demand Factor
  • CO₂ Estimate = kWh × 0.491 kg CO₂/kWh (PH grid, DOE reference — Mindanao grid)
  • HP only entered: Watts = HP × 746

Dashboard Row 2 totals estimated kWh/month and kg CO₂/month across all assets for Green Hospital and PHIC/DOH/PCSS accreditation documentation.
